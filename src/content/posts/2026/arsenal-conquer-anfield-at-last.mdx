Fourteen years. That is how long it had been since we last walked out of Anfield with all three points, way back in 2012 when Mikel Arteta was actually starting in our midfield. Today, Arteta finally broke that curse as our manager with a gritty 1 to 0 victory over Liverpool. This win puts us three points clear at the top of the Premier League, leapfrogging Manchester City while maintaining our eight point cushion over Chelsea and five points over Brighton. Even better, it opens a massive nine point gap over Liverpool after just nine games.

But honestly, if you only looked at our group chats before halftime, you would have thought we were losing by four.

I really thought Arteta would start Gyokeres up front after our awful display against Fleetwood earlier this week... until I saw Kai Havertz leading the line on the teamsheet. Why stick with Havertz when he looked so sluggish and toothless just days ago? I had criticized him heavily after Fleetwood because it felt like the fight simply wasn't there, and I felt he owed our fanbase a massive performance today. I was wrong to doubt Arteta's call. And I am truly glad I was!

Don't get me wrong, our attackers still struggled to give us a proper outlet against Liverpool's pressure, especially with Joe Gomez having a brilliant game at the back for them. Yet in the 17th minute, Havertz read the space at the back post and buried the winning goal when it mattered most. He followed that finish up by leaving everything on the pitch and heading away endless crosses inside our own box, including one crucial clearance right off the head of Araujo when Liverpool looked certain to score. At full time, Havertz even told the cameras that 95 percent of the goal belonged to Declan Rice. You can critique aspects of his overall game, whereas you can never question his humility or how much he fights for this club.

As for Declan Rice, what more is there left to say? Jamie Carragher called him the best midfielder in the Premier League after the match, and it felt downright poetic watching him dominate at Anfield while Graeme Souness was no doubt watching. Souness has spent months reducing Rice to a limited profile, claiming on television that he drops too deep between the center backs and lacks the technical quality to start for European giants like Real Madrid or Bayern Munich. Today, Rice tore that lazy narrative to pieces. Bruno picked up the ball centrally and fed Noni Madueke, who slipped a pass with perfect weight into the right channel so Rice could whip a sensational ball across the face of goal without even breaking stride.

For far too long, we restricted Rice by forcing him out wide on the left touchline where he couldn't naturally open up on his right foot. Ever since Myles Lewis Skelly came into the team against Fulham back in April, and now with Bruno sitting deeper so Rice can surge ahead whenever Odegaard pushes up, we have finally unlocked those potent right channel runs that we saw glimpses of against Manchester City and Bayern Munich.

Then came the storm.

From the first whistle to the 25th minute, we controlled the tempo and restricted Liverpool to zero shots and just four touches in our box. From the 26th minute onwards, Liverpool threw the kitchen sink at us. 17 shots. 44 touches inside our box. And yet, zero shots on target. ZERO!

Arsenal fans online were panicking about why we dropped deep after taking the lead, as if Liverpool were just going to roll over at Anfield in a six pointer. Of course they were going to ramp up the tempo with their crowd roaring them on, especially since even Manchester City couldn't get control at Anfield until Liverpool had a man sent off. Yes, we rode our luck before the break when Wirtz rattled the crossbar and Mac Allister headed over, not to mention Van Dijk nodding a ball dangerously close to Isak while Raya and Rice got into a brief tangle. However, outside of that chaotic half hour before the interval and a brief ten minute burst when Rio came on, we handled over 50 minutes of this match with complete calm.

The reason: every single player refused to yield an inch. Gabriel and Saliba were rocks at the back while Raya claimed crosses cleanly all afternoon without having to make a single save. Noni Madueke put in an unbelievable shift on the wing, retaining the ball smartly and tracking all the way back to the far post to cover Jurrien Timber before Calafiori and MLS came on to calm things down in the second half. Even Odegaard, who misplaced plenty of simple passes on an off day with the ball, ran himself into the ground so we wouldn't get overrun in the middle of the park.

In fairness, Liverpool have every right to feel aggrieved about the officiating because the pull on Isak inside our box was a stonewall penalty. Even the young lad Nyoni somehow escaped a booking after lunging in during the opening minute and later clattering into Havertz. Still, the reason Isak didn't get that penalty exposes a massive problem with how football is being refereed right now. Isak stayed honest and barely complained to the referee, so VAR simply brushed it aside.

To make matters worse, we saw the exact flip side of that broken logic with Max Dowman against Fleetwood earlier this week. Dowman took an outrageous tackle, refused to roll around feigning injury, and got hit with a three game domestic ban that the independent panel refused to overturn just because he used his leg to nudge the player off him. When officials punish honest reactions like Dowman's and ignore fouls on honest forwards like Isak, they actively train players to dive. That is why we had the embarrassing sight of Gabriel clutching his face after minimal contact from Isak, or Mac Allister throwing himself to the turf pretending Madueke had headbutted him before slapping the back of Odegaard's head without getting punished.

Ultimately, walking out of Anfield with a nine point lead over Liverpool feels monumental. Two years ago in November, when we failed to beat Liverpool and fell seven points behind them, I said a seven point deficit was too big to overcome. Now the tables have flipped and we hold a nine point advantage. Having finished second in the Premier League for three consecutive seasons and having seen Manchester City reel in an eight point gap in the past, I know better than to say the title race is over after nine games. We have the squad and the grit to finally get it over the line, unless we let complacency creep in after a statement win like this.

For tonight, though, we deserve to soak it all in.

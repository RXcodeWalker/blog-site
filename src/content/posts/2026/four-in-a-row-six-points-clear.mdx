Four wins on the spin in the Premier League, six points clear of Manchester City heading into the international break, and another clean sheet at the Emirates. On paper, beating Hull 1-0 after beating Leeds, Forest, and Liverpool should feel like a routine day at the office. Considering we played 11 of our 16 games before this one away from home, grinding out another three points at the end of a grueling schedule is massive.

But honestly, it shouldn't have been this tense. Watching Arsenal right now is exhausting because we never seem to kill games off when we have the chance.

Take the first half against Hull. For the opening 30 minutes, we weren't even on top; they were asking David Raya real questions and causing us genuine problems. Our best moment before the break wasn't a slick passing move, it was a VAR penalty check after Noble Mendy pulled Kai Havertz's shirt. Dale Johnson at the BBC pointed out that because Havertz hadn't actually touched the ball or challenged for it yet, the foul technically took precedence over the offside, just like Fabian Schar against Bournemouth a couple of seasons ago. Fair enough if that's the letter of the law, and maybe we were slightly hard done by, but in my opinion, if you're standing in an offside position when you draw a foul, it's rightly disallowed. Either way, relying on technicalities to bail out a sluggish first half isn't a good look.

Part of the issue was our left side just didn't click. Trossard really struggled because this wasn't a game where we had settled possession in the opposition's half for him to pick clever little passes inside. When the game opened up and got more direct, his lack of blistering pace showed, and transition moments kept falling apart between him and Havertz. Bringing on Gyokeres and Merino in the second half was the right call from Arteta, even if Rice wasn't at his best and Merino's cameo felt a bit neither here nor there. Hincapie coming on for Calafiori went under the radar too, as he was front-footed, won the ball back brilliantly, and instantly had a better understanding with Eze down that flank than Calafiori and Trossard managed all afternoon.

As for Noni Madueke, he finally looked like a proper, dangerous touchline winger again. He took his fullback on, retained the ball well, and it was his drift onto his left foot and spilled shot that allowed Martin Odegaard to force home the winner.

And yet, we still left goals on the pitch.

Late on, Gyokeres took the ball down, flicked it into Eze, who found Merino for a gorgeous lay-off to Madueke. It was the best piece of football we've played all season... and Noni overthought it. Instead of setting himself and burying it into the far corner, he tried to go around the keeper and got tackled. Just like that, we were back to hanging on for dear life until the final whistle.

This has become a glaringly obvious pattern over the last couple of seasons. People calling us "boring" are falling back on lazy analysis, because we play good football, but we never put teams to the sword. It is never job done by the 60th minute. Whether it was being 2-0 up in Prague and still ending up on the back foot, or missing chances to bury Liverpool at Anfield last week, every single match becomes a 90-minute stress test for both the players and our fanbase. I've always backed Arteta because he dragged us from mid-table mediocrity into genuine title contenders, and to his credit, he refuses to make excuses about this brutal schedule. Being this defensively resilient while pressing non-stop makes it tough to be lethal at the other end, and clearly, this formula is making us title winners.

Ultimately, taking the 1-0 and running before the international break is all that matters in the table. Three points are three points, especially against a well-organized Hull side that went to Stamford Bridge and got a draw. Still, if we keep leaving sitters on the pitch instead of killing games off early, we are going to keep playing with fire.

I'm delighted with the win, even if my nerves are completely shot.

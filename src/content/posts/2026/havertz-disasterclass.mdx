Earlier this season, when Arsenal lost 3-0 to Brighton in the Premier League, I was surprisingly calm about the whole thing. We got completely battered, and it was humiliating to watch, yet I just sat there thinking that off days happen over a long campaign. Tonight against Fleetwood Town was a completely different story. We crashed out in the fourth round of the Carabao Cup to a team sitting 18th in League Two, and I can't pretend I'm not furious.

But honestly, from Arsenal's perspective, my anger has way less to do with the result itself than the appalling performances we saw from our senior players. Don't get me wrong, full credit goes to Fleetwood because they parked the bus in a stubborn 5-4-1 formation and fought for their lives. Still, considering how much expensive talent Mikel Arteta put out on that pitch, we had more than enough quality to get the job done without needing penalties. Instead, several first-team regulars played like they couldn't be bothered, whereas the teenagers were the only ones actually trying to make things happen.

The chaos started early on after a bizarre eight-minute delay caused by a drone flying over the stadium. Right after play resumed, Fleetwood went ahead, and while some fans might point fingers at sixteen-year-old Marley Salmon for getting caught on the wrong side, I actually think he did well to recover and push the attacker wide. The real blame falls on Illan Meslier. For a goalkeeper who has played Premier League football, getting beaten right through your legs from that angle is just so disappointing.

What makes the loss even more frustrating is that we weren't even completely toothless in our build-up play. In fact, our crossing into the box was pretty good, especially from Ceadach O'Neill on his debut and Max Dowman. O'Neill looked a bit nervous in the first half, which is understandable since nobody really knew much about him before tonight, yet he came alive after the break and kept taking on his fullback. It was his cross that eventually allowed Viktor Gyökeres to bundle the ball over the line for our equalizer. Even with all those dangerous balls whipped in by Piero Hincapié and Jurriën Timber, our finishing in front of goal was pathetic. We missed so many free headers that it honestly felt like that drone was shining a laser straight into our players' eyes.

And that brings me to Kai Havertz, who is at the absolute center of my anger tonight. I promise I'm not exaggerating when I say that was pound-for-pound one of the worst performances I have ever seen from an Arsenal player. In the past, I have tried so hard to defend him and see what Arteta sees in him, whether it's his movement to create overloads or his big goals against Manchester City and Chelsea. Today, however, he had zero excuses. He wasn't forced to play as a lone striker with his back to goal because Gyökeres was dealing with the center-backs, and he didn't have heavy defensive duties like Martín Zubimendi either. He was playing in his best role, drifting into the right pocket with total freedom against fourth-tier defenders, and every single attack that touched his boots just died.

Every first touch was heavy, and every time he tried to drive forward with the ball, he ran straight into trouble. You know the saying about the straw that broke the camel's back? Tonight wasn't a straw; it was a thirty-ton weight dropped right on top of it. For a player reportedly earning around £275,000 a week whom the manager loves unconditionally, looking worse than League Two opposition is unacceptable. In my opinion, he has fallen way down the pecking order behind our other attackers, and with his contract situation coming up, Arsenal have a massive problem on their hands because he is nowhere near the level of Bukayo Saka, Declan Rice, or William Saliba.

To make matters worse, the end of the match was a complete mess, starting with Max Dowman's straight red card. Fleetwood's number four flew in with an overly aggressive scissor tackle that easily could have injured him, and all Dowman did in response was gently lean his hip into the guy. Sending off a sixteen-year-old for that when the initial foul was ten times more dangerous is a joke. I also really didn't like seeing Arteta give Dowman a public telling-off on the touchline right afterward. I've always backed Arteta for turning us from a struggling mid-table team into constant title contenders, and maybe he thinks tough love is what the kid needs, yet publicly humiliating a teenager when you barely had a clear view from the bench just looked terrible. Then, when we finally reached the penalty shootout, Bruno Guimarães stepped up with that stupid hop-skip-jump stutter run-up, and the second I saw him do it, I knew we were losing the tie.

Ultimately, I find it criminal that our senior players threw away a genuine chance at silverware before November even begins. We have been agonizingly close to major trophies for the last few seasons, so we can't just brush off the Carabao Cup when our squad is this stacked. Cup upsets are part of why people love football, and Fleetwood deserved their moment, though from our side, this performance was an embarrassment. In my opinion, Arteta has to stop giving certain favourites a free pass and start demanding way more ruthlessness in front of goal, because unless our senior attackers wake up soon, nights like this will keep costing us when it matters most.

Arsenal managed to bounce back from the Brighton loss with a hard-fought 2-1 win at home against Leeds today. Six gameweeks into the season, getting three points on the board right after an international break is ultimately what matters most.

However, looking past the result, the performance itself leaves us with a lot to reflect on -- and it reinforced the exact same concerns about our attacking efficiency that have lingered for a while now. For the first 60 minutes, Leeds had us on the ropes and created the better chances, much like Brighton did. We used to complain endlessly about struggling to break down teams that park the bus in a low block, thinking that whenever opponents opened up against us, we would kill them off. Yet here we are, facing two teams in Brighton and Leeds that played open, honest football from the start, and we still struggled to punish them. Teams have realized since halfway through last season that the best form of defense against Arsenal is simply to attack and press us aggressively. They shut down a midfield that wants to play out from the back -- because even if we do beat the press, who are we actually playing out to?

That brings us directly to Arteta's starting lineup, which was baffling to say the least. With Trossard out injured, seeing Madueke start on the left ahead of Eze was a massive surprise. Does that make Eze our third-choice left winger now? And when Merino comes on for Ødegaard later in the game -- even factoring in that we were protecting a 2-1 lead -- it makes you wonder where Eze actually stands in the pecking order. Third or fourth-choice number 10 behind Max Dowman too? He was told to warm up when we went 1-0 down after James Justin's top-corner finish, only to be told to sit right back down once Calafiori bundled home our equalizer off Trafford's mistake.

And it's a very similar story up front. Havertz brushed off his injury concerns and slotted straight back in at number nine, despite Gyökeres coming off four goals in four for Sweden. In fairness, we've all been caught in that cycle with our strikers: watch Havertz for a few games and you want Gyökeres, watch Gyökeres for a few games and you want Havertz back. Neither has completely seized the shirt. Still, against a high-pressing Leeds side leaving space in behind, you'd think we would want a striker with pace who sniffs out balls over the top -- and for all of Havertz's qualities, he simply isn't that profile. It forces you to look back at our recruitment and ask how we ended up in a position where the manager clearly doesn't trust marquee assets bought just 12 months ago.

Don't get me wrong, it's not as if starting Eze or Gyökeres automatically fixes everything, because the deeper issue is a lack of magic, clinical finishing, and ruthlessness across the entire frontline. Madueke was arguably our best attacker on the pitch today -- putting in two dangerous crosses in the first half and setting up a golden chance that Ødegaard completely fluffed in the second -- and even his overall display was average at best. On transitions, our final pass was repeatedly too short, like Saka underhitting a simple ball to Madueke on a promising counter.

Even worse was what happened late in the game when Leeds ran out of ideas and we had the chance to put the match to bed. Gyökeres came off the bench and actually fed Saka clean through one-on-one, and Saka completely missed it. Even if Saka isn't always giving you dominant 90-minute performances anymore, he is supposed to be our "moments" player -- the guy who buries those chances when it counts. Seeing him flash a finish like that was unacceptable. Between that miss, a late Gyökeres chance, and Calafiori consistently getting into better attacking positions than our actual forwards, we easily should have won this game 3-1 or 4-1 instead of sweating it out until the final whistle.

Thankfully, two players made sure those missed chances didn't cost us points. First, David Raya proved once again why he is arguably our most valuable player. His instinctive, point-blank save right at the stroke of halftime kept the score at 0-0; if Kepa is in goal for half our games this season, we probably draw or lose the majority of them.

The second, of course, was Bruno Guimarães. Six games into the Premier League season, and he has already won us two matches off the bench -- first a peach against Sunderland, and now another clinical finish today off a lovely flick from Declan Rice. While fans were calling for MLS to come into the midfield after Brighton to help us handle the press, he didn't quite manage to get us out of those tight scenarios today. The midfield only truly clicked once Bruno stepped onto the pitch. It wasn't just the winning goal; it was the cuteness in his game -- the little nutmegs, dropping the shoulder, the one-two passes -- that shifted our tempo and immediately allowed Ødegaard to step his performance up two or three gears.

Ultimately, I have to caveat all of this by giving Arteta the respect he deserves. We are the reigning Premier League champions predominantly because of him -- because of the structure, the shape, and the defensive solidity he built, rather than any single attacker carrying us. However, it's worth remembering that we only got over the line in the final few games last season when we should have wrapped the title up much earlier, and our toothless attack was the exact reason it got so close. Seeing the same lineup hesitations and the same lack of ruthlessness in Gameweek 6 shows this isn't a new problem. Last season, bringing MLS into the side gave us the spark we needed to get it over the line. This season, unless our forwards start taking accountability in front of goal and Arteta figures out how to get the best out of the squad he assembled, our attack runs the risk of undoing all the brilliant structural work behind it.

Hopefully, we find that missing spark soon.

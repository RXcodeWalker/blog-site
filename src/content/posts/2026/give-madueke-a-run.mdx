Going away to Nottingham Forest and coming back with three points and a clean sheet is never easy. When Noni Madueke drifted inside from the left wing in the 80th minute and rifled a deflected strike high into the roof of the net, it felt like a massive relief. For fans watching across the world, staying up late or waking up hours early, that second-half winner made a tough watch worth it. We have now won every game this season except Brighton away, sitting six points clear of Liverpool and five ahead of Chelsea.

But honestly, let's not pretend that performance was entertaining, or that our attack is anywhere near good enough right now. Before Madueke decided to force the issue on 80 minutes, we hadn't registered a single shot on target. ZERO!

And yet, Noni stepped up once again. Over the last eight days, he has quietly become the only attacker making things happen for us. First, he rolled a neat pass into space to assist Jurrien Timber in midweek. Then, he whipped a dangerous ball to Ben White at the back post against Leeds to set up Calafiori's vital equalizer. Now, he has delivered a match-winning goal when we were desperate for someone to just have a go. When we signed him, I doubted he would have a significant impact, and most of our fanbase viewed him as nothing more than a Premier League-proven backup for Bukayo Saka.

It seems I was wrong, and I'm glad about that!

Don't get me wrong, though: I am not suddenly saying Madueke is the finished article. In fact, for the majority of his time here since we signed him last season, he simply hasn't been good enough. He has been frustratingly inconsistent across so many games, and his finishing in front of goal has been poor for over a year. How many times have we watched him beat his man with pace only to sky his shot into the stands or make the wrong final decision? Not to mention how he would just dribble across the touchline and straight off the pitch... It is only over this recent stretch of games that he is finally starting to put it together and play well, which is why we need to see if he can actually sustain this level rather than getting carried away.

To make matters worse for the rest of our frontline, Madueke is shining at a time when everyone else in attack is struggling. Kai Havertz had a terrible game again. That is now four Premier League away games in a row, across Brighton, Villa, Sunderland, and Forest, where he has given us nothing. No hold-up play, no link-up play, no running the channels. Saka had a few bright moments in the first half before tailing off, whereas Eze had some lovely touches mixed with moments that were genuinely shocking before getting hooked at half-time. At least Gyokeres held the ball up well and ran the channels properly when he came on, and Merino added good control off the bench.

Speaking of Eze, it is honestly so disappointing to watch him struggle because I had such high hopes for him. Hearing his journey from being released by Arsenal as a boy, working part-time at Tesco, grinding through grassroots football, and finally earning his dream return to his boyhood club made him impossible not to root for. After seeing him dominate games for Crystal Palace, I genuinely thought he would be the Premier League-proven X factor to elevate our frontline, but beyond a few fleeting moments of magic, he has been far too inconsistent on the wing to give us what we desperately need.

As for the rest of the pitch, it is almost unfair how much our toothless final third is overshadowing a phenomenal team performance. Think of this team in four sections: our goalkeeper is brilliant, our defence is brilliant, our midfield is very good, and our attack is horrible. David Raya swept up everything calmly and dealt with every ball into the box. In front of him, Gabriel and Mosquera were unbelievable, sweeping up in the first half so Calafiori could roam and Ben White could overlap, then throwing themselves into last-ditch challenges when Forest chased the game through Delap, Hudson-Odoi, Murillo, and Chris Wood. Honestly, we are not missing William Saliba right now, which is the highest praise I can possibly give Mosquera. In midfield, Odegaard quietly cooked, Bruno showed great feet in tight spaces before shifting to the six, and Declan Rice anchored everything brilliantly.

However, this brings me to my genuine gripe with our summer transfer window and Mikel Arteta's attack. I've always backed Arteta because he lifted us from a struggling mid-table side into regular title contenders, and winning every match bar one is no small feat. But going into this season without properly upgrading and reinforcing our frontline was nothing short of gross negligence. After watching our squad grind themselves into the ground last season, leaving ourselves short of clinical attacking options is already catching up to us. The worst part is that opposition sides aren't even scared of us anymore; they don't feel the need to sit in a desperate low block because they can see how blunt we are, actively pressing us and believing they can take points. We shouldn't be relying on desperate defensive heroics and last-ditch tackles just to squeeze past Nottingham Forest.

Ultimately, three points at Nottingham Forest is huge, but grinding out tight wins like this cannot be our long-term plan. Last season, our defence and individual moments got us over the line, but we can't keep relying on last-ditch tackles while our attack offers nothing. Man City are finding ways to win despite their own issues, and if we keep leaving the door open, they will pull away. Madueke stepped up, made the difference, and earned his chance to start. Now, Arteta and the rest of our frontline need to wake up and figure it out.

Before our toothless attack costs us the title. Again.

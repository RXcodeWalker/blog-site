Manchester City are in absolute disarray right now. After starting the season with seven straight wins under Enzo Maresca, they have just lost three Premier League games on the spin: away at Villa Park, at home to Brighton, and now battered physically at Nottingham Forest, all while their former striker Liam Delap turned into prime Cristiano Ronaldo and scored twice against them. Add in the looming Premier League sanctions hanging over the club, and Arsenal are suddenly sitting six points clear heading into the international break.

But honestly, the best part of watching Manchester City fall apart is how completely it exposes the lazy narrative about Arsenal being "boring."

When Pep Guardiola, Rodri, Bernardo Silva, and John Stones all left, I genuinely wondered if Maresca might get a bit of an Arne Slot effect at the Etihad. For seven games, it looked like tweaking a talented core and throwing £400 to £500 million at the squad might keep them in the title race. Then the new manager bounce wore off, Gianluigi Donnarumma started making errors like he did against Leipzig, and the glaringly obvious flaws in their summer midfield rebuild came crashing to the surface.

Look at the money Manchester City spent to replace their midfield. They dropped £125 million on Enzo Fernandez, a player their own fans are relieved to see subbed off because playing him in his best role crowds out the exact spaces Cherki needs to operate in. Then you have Elliot Anderson, who got caught out for Nicolas Jackson's goal at Villa Park and made another costly error in the buildup to Forest's opener. When you spend that kind of money, you expect guaranteed quality, not rawness. Declan Rice cost £105 million and wins those duels ten times out of ten, whereas City's midfield has been getting shrugged off the ball for five or six games straight.

As for the "Arsenal are boring" crowd, look at what happened when two title contenders went to the City Ground. When we went to Nottingham Forest a few weeks ago, people complained we weren't flashy enough, yet we controlled the match, shut down Delap and Morgan Gibbs-White, and handled business with zero drama. Manchester City turned up trying to be cute, neat, and front-footed with an out-and-out number ten, and Oliver Glasner's Forest side physically bullied them off the pitch. Even Liverpool, after spending a fortune on their attack, have a lower xG and fewer goals than we do this season.

It feels almost surreal watching the title race right now, like playing a tennis match against an injured opponent who is clearly going to retire before the final set. Between the independent panel's verdict, the appeal, and the possibility of a massive points deduction or relegation, nobody even knows if City will still be standing by May. Yet regardless of what happens off the pitch, they simply do not look like a team built to survive a Premier League title race on it.

I've always backed Mikel Arteta because he built a team with real steel and solidity on and off the ball, which is exactly how we beat Pep's City by seven clear points to win the league last season. Being ruthless and defensively structured isn't boring; it is what wins silverware while big-spending rivals get exposed.

To sum it all up, Manchester City's meltdown is a massive boost for us, and the rest of the football world is going to enjoy every single slip-up after years of smugness from their fanbase. Still, we cannot afford to take our foot off the gas or assume sanctions will do the job for us, because Brighton face Brentford and could trim our lead to five points.

We just have to keep winning what is in front of us, before City somehow find a way back into this race.

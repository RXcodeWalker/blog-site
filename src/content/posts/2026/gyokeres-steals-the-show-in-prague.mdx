Exactly one year ago on the fourth of November 2025, Arsenal travelled to face Slavia Prague and walked away with a comfortable 3-0 victory thanks to a Bukayo Saka penalty and a Mikel Merino brace. Fast forward twelve months, and we found ourselves back in Prague right after a gruelling battle at Anfield, grinding out a 2-1 win to make it three wins from four in the Champions League league phase. Considering our only dropped points came in an away draw against Bayern Munich, our European record so far is about as close to perfect as it gets.

But honestly, this performance was nowhere near as complete as our trip here last season. We went from looking completely in control during an electric first half to enduring a sluggish, nervy slog after the break. Despite holding 56% possession, we actually got outshot 14 to 11 by Slavia Prague, with almost all of their pressure coming in that chaotic second period.

We made life way harder for ourselves than it needed to be.

Before getting into that second-half drop-off, though, we need to talk about the starting eleven and the main man of the night. Mikel Arteta made six changes from the side that started at Anfield, bringing in Ben White, Piero Hincapie, Martin Zubimendi, Mikel Merino, Eberechi Eze, and Viktor Gyokeres. I expected rotation after the weekend, even though I thought Martin Odegaard might keep his spot or Bruno Guimaraes would step in to give Declan Rice a rest. Still, seeing Zubimendi in the lineup was a huge positive, and the rotated front line wasted no time making an impact.

As for Viktor Gyokeres, he ran the show in the first half with a goal and an assist. Let's not pretend he is having a sensational campaign overall, because he has clearly struggled for consistency this season. However, one specific part of his game that deserves massive credit is his awareness of space and the runners around him. Time and time again, with his back to goal, he has pulled off clever flicks to knock the ball into space for our wingers, just like he did for Eze against Everton and for Kai Havertz against Brighton. Whenever people compare our striker role to Roberto Firmino, I find it an insult to Firmino since nobody at Arsenal has looked remotely close to that level of false 9 play since Gabriel Jesus at his peak in 2022. Yet when it comes to pure hold-up play and instinctive flicks over the top, Gyokeres is executing that role far better than Havertz right now.

That exact skill gave us our opening goal. Gyokeres pulled off a brilliant flick over the top to put Noni Madueke through on goal, and instead of overcomplicating things by shifting the ball onto his favoured left foot, Madueke struck it early with his right. The goalkeeper probably should have done a bit better, as it wasn't right in the corner, but Madueke took it clinically to give us the early lead.

Then came our second goal before halftime, and the team buildup leading up to it was just as impressive as the finish. It started with David Raya playing a sharp pass into Gyokeres, who produced another lovely flick into Hincapie before our central progression broke down. Slavia Prague briefly recovered the ball, only for Merino to step in immediately and force a turnover in midfield, which opened up the pitch for Ben White to fizz a pass into the feet of our striker. Gyokeres exchanged a quick one-two with Merino inside the box, waited for the sliding defender to fly past him, and curled a peach of a shot in off the far post.

Two goals up. Total control. It should have been game over by halftime.

In fact, we could have easily been 3-0 up before the interval if Merino had converted a deflected right-footed effort or buried a close-range header that Gyokeres nodded back across goal for what would have been his second assist. Instead, we came out for the second half lacking cohesion and game management, allowing Slavia Prague to pin us back with crosses and corners. Raya had to pull off a phenomenal save to keep them out before they eventually scored a late goal they thoroughly deserved, which completely ruined any chance of Arteta bringing on William Saliba or giving Max Dowman more than a couple of minutes at the end.

Don't get me wrong, I have always backed Arteta for transforming us from a struggling mid-table side into consistent contenders, and getting the job done away in Europe right after winning at Anfield deserves applause. But I genuinely cannot understand his squad management and midfield tweaks in the second half. Why did Gyokeres have to come off for Odegaard just so Merino could play a cameo up front? Why was Declan Rice moved out of the number six position where he has been thriving all season, only to be forced to bomb up and down the pitch in a box-to-box role he hasn't played since mid-April? Even with Zubimendi starting at the base of midfield, Rice and Merino could have easily balanced the duties ahead of him without running Rice into the ground for a full 90 minutes when Bruno Guimaraes was sitting right there on the bench.

Rice looked completely shattered by the final whistle.

Ultimately, walking away from Prague with all three points keeps us in a commanding position in the Champions League table, and seeing Gyokeres play with so much confidence on the ball is a massive boost going forward. We have seen over the last three seasons how fine the margins are when chasing major silverware, and burning out our most important midfielder in matches that should be wrapped up by halftime is a dangerous game to play. Arsenal have the squad depth to rotate properly now, and Arteta needs to start using it to protect key players like Rice.

Before exhaustion costs us later in the season.
